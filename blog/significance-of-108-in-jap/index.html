Understanding the Significance of 108 in Jap Practice

By Naam Jap Editorial Team • Updated November 26, 2026

Across Hindu, Buddhist, and yogic meditation practices, a standard mala consists of 108 counting beads plus a central Guru (or Sumeru) bead that marks the start and end of a round.

Why 108 Is Widely Used in Mantra Repetition

Multiple Indian traditions recognize 108 as a sacred, balanced number of completeness:

  • Cultural & Linguistic Traditions: The Sanskrit alphabet has 54 letters (each considered to carry masculine and feminine aspects, totaling 108), and 108 appears across classical astronomy (27 lunar constellations across 4 padas) and sacred temple/text traditions.
  • Practical Meditative Rhythm: One round of 108 short mantra repetitions takes roughly 2 to 6 minutes—long enough for the mind to settle beyond initial restlessness, yet short enough to repeat daily.
  • Traditional 100 + 8 Buffer: Some lineages explain 108 as 100 complete repetitions plus 8 extra counts to compensate for minor pronunciation slips or moments of distraction.

Other Common Jap Targets

When time is limited or when taking a deeper daily sankalpa, practitioners also use smaller or larger counts such as 11, 21, 51, 216 (2 malas), 501, or 1008.
